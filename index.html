About your editor

I'm Ben, and I read the way most people can't switch off: the misplaced apostrophe on the pub menu, the character whose eyes change colour in chapter nine, the sentence that would sing if two words swapped places. For years that was just a mildly annoying habit. Now it's the job.

Before this I worked at The Oxbridge Formula, helping applicants prepare for Oxbridge entrance exams and the personal statements that go with them. That's where the academic side of this work started. It's also where I learned how fast a reader stops believing a sentence that claims something it hasn't shown.

My approach is simple: your voice, made stronger. A good edit should leave a manuscript sounding more like its author, not more like its editor, so I explain every change I suggest and I never make one silently. You'll always know what I did and why.

You're also dealing with me rather than an agency. The person who answers your first email is the person who does the editing, and the one who replies when you query something six weeks later. Nothing gets subcontracted, so nothing comes back in a house style that isn't yours.

I'm also honest to a fault about what you actually need. If your draft needs structural work, I'll tell you before you pay for a polish it isn't ready for – and if it doesn't need me at all, I'll tell you that too.

When I'm not editing, I set and solve Ximenean cryptic crosswords, which is possibly the only hobby that makes proofreading look relaxed.

Manuscript Editing

For novels, novellas, non-fiction, and memoirs. Base prices cover manuscripts up to 60,000 words; longer works are priced per additional 1,000 words so neither of us gets a surprise.

Proofreading£250 · then £4 per 1,000 words

Typically the last stage of editing, and your final opportunity for corrections before the manuscript goes out into the world. Proofreading assumes the heavy lifting is already done. It's the fine-toothed comb, not the restructure.

  • Spelling and punctuation corrected throughout.
  • Sequential page numbers checked, because they go wrong far more often than you'd think.
  • Widows and orphans removed, so no lines are left stranded at the tops and bottoms of pages.
  • Typography and layout inconsistencies identified: rogue fonts, wandering margins, chapter openings that don't match.
  • Contents page cross-checked against actual chapter titles and page numbers.
  • Running heads, headers, and footers checked for consistency.
  • Awkward line breaks and hyphenation errors caught before a reader stumbles on them.

One honest caveat: if your manuscript hasn't been copyedited, a proofread alone may not be the right service – and I'll say so rather than take the money.

Free sample: send me your first 1,000 words and I'll proofread them at no charge, no strings attached.

Copyediting£350 · then £6 per 1,000 words

A sentence-by-sentence edit of the whole manuscript. Where proofreading catches errors, copyediting improves the writing itself. A full proofread comes in as the final pass, so you don't pay for it twice.

  • Every sentence considered on its own merits: word choice, rhythm, clarity.
  • Consistency of style throughout: spelling conventions, hyphenation, capitalisation, numbers, and tense.
  • Factual inconsistencies flagged. The eye colour that changes in chapter nine, the Tuesday that somehow follows a Friday.
  • Readability and accuracy improved without flattening your voice. The aim is more you, not more me.
  • Delivered with tracked changes in Word, so you can accept or reject every single edit.
  • Queries raised as comments rather than silent rewrites. If I'm unsure what you meant, I ask.
  • A style sheet supplied with the edit, recording every decision so future edits and sequels stay consistent.
  • A full proofread included as the final stage.

Developmental / Structural Editing£400 · then £7 per 1,000 words

Big-picture feedback on an early draft, at the stage where line-level polish would be wasted effort. This is a feedback service rather than a hands-on rewrite: an in-depth analysis of the story and a roadmap for the next draft. You stay the author; I tell you honestly what's working and what isn't.

  • In-depth analysis of concept, structure, flow, consistency, and style.
  • Point of view examined: whether it's the right one, and whether it ever slips.
  • Character and dialogue: motivations that hold up under pressure, voices that stay distinct on the page.
  • Plot believability tested, with the places a reader's trust would wobble named specifically.
  • Subplots checked against the main story: each one should enhance it, and I'll flag any that compete with it instead.

This service doesn't include copyediting or proofreading – deliberately. Your draft will change after this feedback, and polishing sentences you're about to delete helps nobody. Once the structure is settled, copyediting is the next step.

Common questions

What's the difference between proofreading, copyediting, and developmental editing?

Three different jobs, done at three different points in a book's life.

  • Developmental editing looks at the book. Structure, pacing, character, whether the thing works at all.
  • Copyediting looks at the sentences. Word choice, clarity, consistency, and the eye colour that changed in chapter nine.
  • Proofreading looks at the page. Typos, punctuation, page numbers.

The shorthand version: developmental editing changes what you wrote. Copyediting changes how you wrote it. Proofreading is the last person in the room before the door closes.

The three are compared in full on a separate page, including the same paragraph edited three ways.

Which kind of editing do I actually need?

Depends where the draft is.

Not sure the story works yet? That's developmental. Structure settled, and you want the prose as good as it can be? Copyediting. Already edited, and you just want a clean pass before it goes out into the world? Proofreading.

Still stuck? Send me the first chapter and a one-line summary. I'll tell you for free, and "you need less than you think" is an answer I give more often than people expect.

How long will my edit take?

Depends on length, service, and what's already in the diary. You'll get a delivery date with the quote, before you've paid anything.

If I can't hit your deadline I'll say so on day one rather than take the booking and hope it works out. A late edit is worse than no edit, particularly if you've got a preorder live.

Do you use AI to edit my work?

No. I read it.

A spellchecker is fine, and I use one like everybody else. But the judgement calls are the entire job. Whether a joke lands. Whether a paragraph is earning its place. Whether that odd phrase is your voice or just a Tuesday afternoon.

Software can't make those calls, and your manuscript doesn't get pasted into a chatbot.

Do you work in British or American English?

Either. Tell me which you want, or tell me which market you're selling into and I'll advise. If most of your readers are American, American conventions are usually right regardless of where you happen to live.

Whichever we settle on goes on your style sheet and gets applied all the way through. Half-and-half spelling is one of the most common things I fix, and almost nobody realises they're doing it.

What file formats do you work in?

Word (.docx) for most editing, with tracked changes so you can accept or reject each edit on its own. Google Docs in suggesting mode if you'd rather. PDF for proofreading typeset page proofs.

Scrivener users: compile to Word first, then bring the edits back in afterwards. Or send it to me for formatting once it's final and skip that step entirely.

Where are you based, and can you work with clients outside the UK?

I'm in the UK and I work with clients anywhere. Editing happens over email and shared files with the occasional call, so nobody has ever needed me physically in the room.

Invoices are in pounds sterling. Time zones have never caused a problem, though I'll be straight with you about when you'll hear back if we're twelve hours apart.
