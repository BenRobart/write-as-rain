You get back a manuscript where the conventions have been treated as errors. The romance beat structure "flattened for pace". The invented names quietly regularised. A clue moved because it looked like a repetition, which it was, because it was a clue.

Three things I do differently, one per genre.

Romance. A series bible, so book six still matches book one: names, timelines, family trees, and where you set the heat level. Yours stays exactly where you put it. I'm editing the book you wrote.

Crime and thriller. A clue timeline, tracking what the reader knows and when, so you can prove the reveal is fair rather than hope it is. UK procedure checked against UK procedure, not against American television.

Fantasy and sci-fi. A glossary built from your own text, so your spellings and your invented words become the standard I hold the manuscript to. Long books priced openly by the 1,000 words, because they're long and pretending otherwise helps nobody.
